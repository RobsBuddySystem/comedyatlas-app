The Bar Room Split That Gave Edinburgh Two Free Fringes

2026-08-19 · COMEDY ATLAS

Peter Buckley Hill invented the pass-the-bucket model for the Edinburgh Fringe in 1996: no cover charge, donations only. Eight years later, Alex Petty and Kevin McCarron used the same idea to launch their own free shows — and became the first to scale it into a second, separate organization, the one that today feeds hundreds of free Edinburgh listings in Comedy Atlas's own database. What happened between them is less a rivalry than two people going for the same destination by different roads, then quietly parting ways.

Two Comedy Club Owners, One Borrowed Idea

Petty and McCarron weren't newcomers — they'd run Laughing Horse as a comedy club brand since the late 1990s. When they saw PBH's model working at the Fringe, they launched their own free shows in 2004: one room, three shows. By year two they'd added a second room and venue, and partnered with PBH under a shared banner.

The Partnership That Didn't Last

The partnership didn't last. Petty summed it up plainly years later: "Fairly soon it was clear we couldn't work with Peter, so decided to go it alone." In 2006 Petty and McCarron ended the partnership; the Free Edinburgh Fringe Festival has operated fully separate from PBH's Free Fringe since 2007. The names split too: PBH kept "Free Fringe," Laughing Horse took "Free Edinburgh Fringe Festival."

The two organizations' public statements suggest a difference in emphasis — PBH's side talks about the free fringe as a movement and a set of principles; Laughing Horse's side talks in venues, rooms, and a business that scales. That's a reading of tone, not a quote, but it tracks with how each grew.

Growth on Two Different Tracks

Both grew. PBH's Free Fringe reported 176 shows across 19 venues by 2009 — roughly 50% year-on-year growth — the same year PBH won the Edinburgh Comedy Awards Panel Prize. Laughing Horse now puts its footprint at over 20 venues and 40-plus performance spaces. The growth wasn't linear: Petty put 2018 output at roughly 9,000 performances across 400 shows, while the organization's own 20th-anniversary count in 2024 was lower — 7,812 performances across 389 shows. Two different years, two different numbers, no clean explanation for the dip.

Kevin McCarron's Legacy

Kevin McCarron stepped away from Laughing Horse about a decade before his death. He'd spent 2001 to 2018 running the club's New Act of the Year competition, an early stop for Greg Davies, Russell Kane, Bobby Mair, Sofie Hagen, and Janine Harouni. He died in June 2026, at 72.

One Bucket, Two Organizations

Nearly two decades on, the fork barely registers to most Fringe-goers. Both organizations still run free shows across dozens of venues every August — the entire ecosystem still runs on the same bucket passed around a single room at Linsay's bar in 2004.
